# sim-spaceflight-immune-gut-brain
Collected data and statistical assessment for the research article, "Simulated spaceflight disrupts immune, intestinal, and neurological homeostasis and drives neuroinflammation, axonal injury, and behavioral deficits"

Authors:
Marissa Burke, Göknur Kara, Morgan Holcomb, Christopher E. Mason, Sonia Villapol

Abstract:
Spaceflight perturbs multiple organ systems, yet the integrated impact of spaceflight-relevant stressors across immune, intestinal, 
and neurological systems remains poorly defined. We employed a ground-based model that combined hindlimb unloading (HU) with low-dose 
ionizing radiation (IR; 50 or 100cGy) to quantify gut and brain pathology, peripheral immune phenotypes, and behavioral performance 
in male and female C57BL/6 mice. HU and/or IR induced region-selective neurodegenerative changes consistent with axonal injury. In 
the somatosensory cortex, MAP-2+ neuronal labeling was reduced and SMI-312+ axonal injury increased, while long-range fiber pathways, 
including the corpus callosum, cingulate gyrus, and external capsule, exhibited prominent axonal damage and gliosis. Iba-1+ microglial 
and GFAP+ astrocytic responses were most pronounced following combined HU+100cGy exposure. Blood-brain barrier alterations in cortical 
and hippocampal regions were characterized by reduced claudin-5 labeling and increased parenchymal IgG leakage. Peripheral 
immunophenotyping revealed sustained alterations in innate and adaptive immune populations, including changes in NK+, CD11b+, and T-cell 
populations, with several responses differing between males and females. The jejunum, ileum, and colon exhibited architectural remodeling 
and barrier disruption, including altered mucin profiles, reduced ZO-1 tight-junction labeling, and increased CD45+ leukocyte infiltration.
Behavioral testing revealed alterations across affective and cognitive domains, including increased forced-swim-test immobility, impaired
recognition memory, and less efficient spatial navigation strategies, with the magnitude of selected responses differing by sex. 
Collectively, these findings demonstrate coordinated immune, intestinal, and neurological alterations following simulated spaceflight 
stressors and identify sex as an important biological variable influencing selected responses associated with neurobehavioral dysfunction.
